# Subhasta

Subhasta (Latin *sub hasta*, "under the spear") is a single-file web app about the art trade: auction results, houses and the people who run them, a provenance ledger built from the National Gallery of Art's open data, the taxes, fees and rules that sit under a price, and a members' board. It is a terminal, not a feed: every figure is typed into the data files under `src/` (two more, the globe's dot mask and the Lab's benchmarks, are generated by scripts), carries a verification flag (V verified, S secondary, U unverified) and links its source; the Lab computes its measures from them at render. The build concatenates the data, the engines and the stylesheet into one HTML page (`build/rostrum.html`, about 1.9 MB with 771 provenance records, the world map and the globe inline) that is published as a private Claude artifact together with 117 public-domain pictures and the source tree. There is no framework, no bundler and no server; the page runs inside the artifact host (which blocks external fetches and images) and, with reduced features, from `file://`. See `ARCHITECTURE.md` for how it is put together and `REFRESH.md` for the monthly data refresh.

## The sections

The masthead lists fourteen. A fifteenth view, **Signals**, has no masthead button and is reached from the Floor, the palette, the Reel, hotkey 7 or `#signals`. Every view, dossier, deep-linkable drawer, Lab instrument and Reel card has an address; Back and Forward walk the views and dossiers, and close the Reel onto the page it covered.

| Section | What it shows (as rendered by `src/app.js` and the engines it embeds) |
|---|---|
| **Reel** | A full-screen, auto-advancing sequence of 40 cards (16 kinds: lot, upcoming, story, signal, price path, private treaty, fees, KPI, calendar, undercurrent, journey, money, symptom, hub, segment, closing card) with the pictures; `#reel-N` deep-links a card. Own IIFE in `src/reel.js`. |
| **Floor** | The front page: hero story, the split-flap hammer board (the 12 latest lots), four KPI tiles that open evidence drawers, the "For your desk" lens strip, four vital signs, four undercurrents, the lots coming to the block, a treemap of every lot above $20m, the houses' 2019–2025 bars, country and region bars, the next eight calendar entries, the wire, four signals and the half-year table. |
| **Pulse** | The market as a patient: 16 vital signs with sparklines and source drawers, currency weather (the dollar against sterling, the euro, the franc and the Hong Kong dollar, month-end ECB rates, and what a lot costs across the border), global sales 2009–2025, country shares, online sales, price bands and categories for 2025, 16 diagnoses ("symptoms") with evidence and prognosis, the long view 1744–2026 by era, upcoming dates, the report calendar, museum visitors, 36 artist records, dated dispatches by region; print and CSV export. |
| **Atlas** | The globe — a dot-matrix earth tinted by share of sales with the hubs as beacons, in five modes (share, momentum, journeys of six trophies through the freeport, trade flows when the data is in, the next 45 days) — then a Natural Earth world map (Robinson projection) shaded by 2025 share of sales with 31 hub dots, hub cards and drawers (houses present, fairs, the buyer's rules, top sale, galleries, what is shifting, the local pitfall), 18 regulatory regimes side by side, 24 collecting segments in four groups, 29 institutions, and the year of fairs and sale weeks by month. |
| **Houses** | A directory of 16 auction houses and 51 other players (galleries, advisors, lenders, insurers, fairs, data firms, shippers, freeports) with type chips, search and sort; each opens a dossier: sales since 2019, the year in detail, history timeline, results, stories, facts, leadership, official channels, how to approach, sources. |
| **People** | 96 people grouped by organisation, searchable; "routes in" (careers, internships, press desks) per house; a person page with remit, peers and the organisational channel; "Add to Desk". |
| **Hammer** | 96 marquee lots since 1990 with house, category, period and text filters; an artists index; records by category; reported private-treaty sales. A lot page carries the ownership chain, a log-scale price path, the implied hammer and fee check under each house's 2026 schedule, and "Follow the money for this lot". An artist page carries the public-domain gallery, lots, NGA ownership histories (each with the Lab's 1933–45 chip), the dealers named in those histories (each opens the Ledger on that dealer and artist) and documented chains. |
| **Ledger** | 771 ownership histories of works by 115 artists from the National Gallery of Art's open data (CC0): search, artist filter, a filter by the Lab's reading of 1933–45 (open, quiet, partly accounted for, accounted for, not in scope) with a chip on each record, a dealer lineage of 33 dealers with mention counts (a dealer is matched as a whole name, with its other spellings and without look-alike names, in a history's main text: the Lab's matcher, so the counts agree), six case files; each record opens the full provenance text with footnotes, the Lab's reading of that history and the nga.gov link. |
| **Salon** | 18 stories behind the prices, 24 collectors, a members' board and member cards stored in the artifact's database (read-only messages without the host). |
| **Undertow** | Follow the money: a Sankey of where a buyer's dollar goes under a chosen hammer ($100k to $2bn), house, sale room, structure, seller's deal, destination and financing, with a line-by-line ledger (the artist's resale right by room, payer, threshold, term and nationality), a five-room comparison, the same purchase in eight places to keep it, and a matrix of every room against every destination (the first year in all against the cheapest; the room's VAT comes back when the work is exported or goes into bond, on the house's terms; Geneva charges it on hammer and premium; a museum loan defers the use tax, so it is shown but not ranked); six journeys; the circuit of 16 stations a work passes through; the atlas of 61 undercurrents (28 cases, 33 rules) with filters; the casebook; ten checks before any deal (ticks kept in this browser). |
| **Desk** | The composer (a short e-mail to a named person built from the dossiers, four intents, optional "Refine with Claude"), the outreach pipeline with status, next action and CSV export, the watch list, and the Clinic (six risks scored from a deal's description, with the checks that lower them; export licences tested against each regime's own threshold; a 1933–45 base rate for the named artist). |
| **Lexicon** | A fee calculator for the five schedules the terminal knows, a 36-term glossary with Russian equivalents, the anatomy of a sale, the buyer's premium in force, a career map and the data room of 25 sources and APIs. |
| **Lab** | Six instruments computed from the data at render: what trophies earned (16 repeat sales against the S&P 500 total return, gold and US CPI over the same years, `src/data_bench.js`, with a 90% bootstrap interval for the median), the round trip (Follow the money's entry, a yearly carry, the exit; net IRR, the break-even growth, the same round trip at each repeat sale's own rate, like for like by price, and a break-even grid by lot size and years held), how concentrated the market is (HHI of the big houses, the countries and the named artists, price bands, Gini, value by artist and by decade), provenance 1933 to 1945 (every Ledger history in the museums' research scope — made before 1946, acquired after 1932 — read entry by entry: accounted for, partly accounted for or quiet, where the work starts the period and where the chain opens; the dealers and houses the histories name; country, artist and dealer drill-down; CSV), how long owners held (every holding the histories date at both ends: the collector's and the dealer's median hold, how collectors let go by era, hands before the Gallery, and the dealers who sold to the founders' collections, drawn as a flow; CSV) and how much of the terminal is verified (every V/S/U flag and source address counted), with the limits register: 23 things the models leave out, in seven areas, each linked from the notes that rely on it (`#lab-limits-<area>`); `#lab-<instrument>` deep-links each. |
| **Circle** | The offer — Reader (free), Private and Desk (on request) — seven reasons the terminal is different with counts computed from the data, a request for an invitation sent to the owner as a comment on the page, and the Subhasta Brief: a one-page monthly letter rendered from the data, printable, copyable, saveable as Markdown. |
| *Signals* | 11 momentum readings with evidence and direction, the wire, the calendar to mid-2027 and the RSS feeds. |

## Quick start

```
npm install            # jsdom (test), axe-core and Playwright (runtime audit); Python 3 needs no packages for the build
npm run check          # build → test → lint → globals map; the four gates every change must pass
npm run check:full     # the same plus the runtime audit (≈2 min, Chromium) and the Ledger rebuild check
```
The scripts, one by one (`package.json`):

| Script | Runs | What it enforces |
|---|---|---|
| `npm run build` | `python3 build.py` | assembles `build/rostrum.html` (the artifact page) and `build/preview.html` (browser wrapper); counts the collections by evaluating the data in Node; refuses a missing or doubled placeholder, a `</script` in pasted text, a zero count or an unreplaced token |
| `npm test` | `node test.js` | jsdom smoke test over `preview.html`: every view, drawer, dossier, the Reel, deep links, hash restore, search; exits 1 unless the last line is `errors: none` |
| `npm run lint` | `node tools/lint_data.js --quiet` | the data-layer lint: ids, every cross-reference, images, flags, dates, URLs, the engine-facing contracts (`HUBS[].room`, `ARTIST_DIED`, `REGIMES` booleans, `HERO`, `FLOOR`, `NEWS[].dt` …); exits 1 on ERROR |
| `npm run globals` | `python3 tools/globals.py --no-tables` | who declares and who uses every top-level name across the concatenated files; fatal collisions fail |
| `npm run audit` | `node tools/audit_runtime.js` | Playwright + axe-core: errors in four viewport/theme contexts, overflow at 320, 390 and 1400 px, the masthead at 1280–1920 px, 475 deep links, keyboard and focus, accessibility, reduced motion, storage failure, performance; exits 1 on any failure |
| `npm run shots` | `node tools/shots.js` | desktop and phone screenshots of the main views into `shots/` |
| `npm run ledger:check` | `python3 tools/nga_subset.py --check` | rebuilds `data/nga_subset.json` from the NGA CSVs and `data/nga_ids.txt` and checks it is byte-identical |

To preview locally, open `build/preview.html` in a browser: the pictures resolve from `build/img/`, Google Fonts load if the machine is online, and everything that needs the artifact host (the Salon board, the pipeline store, CSV saving, "Refine with Claude") degrades to a message or a clipboard copy. `test.js`, `audit_runtime.js` and `shots.js` run against `build/preview.html`, so build first. The runtime audit expects Chromium at `/opt/google/chrome/chrome` (override with `CHROME=…`). `tools/nga_subset.py` needs the two NGA CSVs (about 170 MB) in `data/`; `--fetch` downloads them.

The tree is a git repository (`main`; first commit "Subhasta v14 baseline"). It has no remote yet: the artifact's published `src/…` files are the copy a fresh session can reach, so every editing session republishes them (see Publishing).

## Publishing

The page is `build/rostrum.html`, published with the Artifact tool to the existing artifact URL so that the address and the 234 picture files are kept; the whole source tree (sources, tools, docs, audits, data registries) is republished alongside it under `src/…` so that a fresh session can rebuild it. The exact `files` map, the `root`, and the content-type rules (`.py` scripts and `page.html` go as `text/plain`) are in `REFRESH.md` §4, which also keeps a `## Versions` ledger of what each artifact version carries. Do not pass `capabilities` on a republish: the stored declaration (db rules for the members' cards and the board, comments for the Circle's invitation, downloads, sample, user profile) is carried forward.

## The monthly refresh

A scheduled task runs on the 3rd of each month in a fresh cloud session. It reads the published source from the artifact (`REFRESH.md` §1), updates `TODAY` and the dated collections (dispatches, calendar, fairs, upcoming lots, news, signals, the hubs and symptoms touched by the month's marquee weeks), rebuilds, tests and republishes (§3–§4). The data map in `REFRESH.md` §2 says which constant holds what; `ARCHITECTURE.md` §7 explains the fields and the order of edits for each kind of addition.

## Conventions

Every figure carries `vf` (or `v` in ATLAS and JOURNEYS) = V/S/U, rendered by `vflag()`; prices include buyer's premium unless stated; dates are ISO; apostrophes inside single-quoted JS strings are the typographic ’; British spelling and short declarative sentences; pictures are public domain only and their captions say whether they show the lot itself ("The lot itself") or a related work ("Related:", "Illustration:").
