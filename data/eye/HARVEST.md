# Standard Ebooks cover harvest for the Eye (v29) — report, 16 Nov 2026

## 1. Catalogue
- Pages fetched: **129 of 129** (`https://standardebooks.org/ebooks?page=N`, 12 links a page, the last with 10); no fetch failed, so `missing_pages.txt` is empty. Every page's links are kept in `pages/pNNN.txt`.
- Links collected: 1,546 lines → **1,180 distinct slugs** in `slugs.txt` (expected ≈1,540). The shortfall is the site's, not the fetches': the newest-first listing sorts on a key with large tie groups, so adjacent page numbers return overlapping 12-item windows (page 10 repeats 6 of page 9, page 108 repeats all of 107 …); the windows are stable between fetches, and any extra parameter (`sort`, `per-page`, `query`) makes the server answer with page 129 marked current, so no other ordering was reachable. About 360 ebooks are therefore not in the sample. The GitHub org listing is closed to this session (API bound to configured repos).

## 2. Harvest
- `se_harvest.py` on 1,180 slugs: **146 s**, 1,169 rows with images, 11 errors (2 × colophon 404, 9 × "colophon not parsed").
- Post-fix (`se_fix.py`, `se_reparse.py`; pool backed up to `out/se_pool.raw.json`): the harvest regex loses an artist whose name is not a link (`<b epub:type="z3998:personal-name">`) and truncates a linked name at an inner `<abbr>` — **160 rows had an empty artist and 22 a one-word artist** ("John" for John R. Neill); all were re-read from the colophons and patched in place, plus three partial year fields (`1854/1869`, `1919–1920`, Twachtman's `c. 1889–1990`).
- `fixes.json`: **10 rows** built from the colophon text for the parse failures (`<abbr>c.</abbr>`, `1818-1819`, "1830 or later", a parenthetical after the title, a `<b>` title, no "a painting" phrase) and for Grimmelshausen, whose repo name is cut to GitHub's 100 characters (`…scrope-goodri`). Their images were fetched the same way as the harvest's. Two of them are "An Unknown Artist" works with the kind unstated (Landscape with a Bull, undated; Look Homeward, Angel, 1929).
- Still an error: `joseph-conrad/heart-of-darkness` — no repo at the derived name on either branch.
- **Pool: 1,179 paintings** (1,169 in `out/se_pool.json` + 10 in `fixes.json`); `out/img`: 1,179 JPEGs, **135 MB** on disk (138.6 MB by row bytes, 18–289 KB each, ≤1000 px; 880 portrait, 286 landscape among the harvest rows). 410 of 1,179 are the 1400×2100 cover crop because `cover.source.jpg` exceeds 3 MB. 1,061 harvest rows carry the colophon's artist link (Wikipedia, a few Art UK), 108 none. Kinds: 1,174 "painting", 2 "illustration" (Betts, Pyle), 3 unstated.

## 3. Places (`places.json`, 702 artist strings)
- **NGA constituents.csv placed 324** (`vf: "V"`): 298 exact, 21 loose (initial-aware, lifespan checked against the painting years), 10 picked by hand where the match was ambiguous (Gainsborough vs. "Mrs. Thomas Gainsborough", Hoppner, West, John Northcote Nash, Antonio Zeno Shindler, Johann Zoffany, O'Brien Inman, Veronese, de László, Roelandt Savery); 5 exact-name matches rejected as namesakes (John Collier the photographer, John Taylor 1738–1814, the sculptor William Edmondson, "Leland Harrison", a nonsense "Henry Murphy 1855–1995"). Nationality, born, died are NGA's; country is mapped from the nationality unless the painter worked elsewhere (56 overrides from memory with a `note`: Cassatt, Whistler, Sargent, Tanner, Chagall, Kandinsky, Alma-Tadema, El Greco, Holbein, van Dyck, Poussin …); city from memory where sure (319 of 324).
- **From memory 353** (`vf: "U"`), with country and, where sure, city and dates; a painter who worked in two countries gets the one where the cover's painting was most likely made, said in `note`.
- **Searched 24** (`vf: "S"`, 25 of 25 searches used): obscure dust-jacket and pulp illustrators. Death years came from the Standard Ebooks artwork pages the searches surfaced (`standardebooks.org/artworks/…`, "d. YYYY"); nationality and dates for three from the Smithsonian American Art Museum and the Akron Art Museum. One name stays unplaced after two searches: **C. Fleming Williams** ("The Jump", 1913).
- `An Unknown Artist` covers 36 works; name variants are kept as separate keys exactly as the colophons write them (two spellings of J. Allen St. John, Shishkin, Rembrandt, Fragonard, Gigoux, Gilbert, Reynolds).

## 4. Licence (`licence.json`, 1,179 slugs)
Rule: painter died ≤ 1955 or work made before 1930 → `both`; else `us-only` when both facts are known; else `unknown`. A work dated only "Nth century" with N ≤ 19 counts as before 1930. **both 1,164 · us-only 10 · unknown 5.** us-only: the 1930 jackets of Tandy (4), Douglas, Hopper's Early Sunday Morning, Schlaikjer, Macadam, Brillhart (1958), Presser (1943). unknown: Herndon (1930, no death year), two undated unknown-artist works, two "20th century" unknown-artist works.

## 5. Odd things
- No colophon in the sample names a photograph or an engraving; everything is "a painting" except two book illustrations and three works with no kind stated.
- Colophon dates that contradict the painter: Goya "1867" (d. 1828), Benjamin Marshall "1886" (d. 1835), Henry Alexander "1856" (b. 1860), Schalcken "1643" (his birth year), Ream "1838–1917" (his lifespan), Twachtman "1990", Andrée Rexroth "1943" (d. 1940). Noted in `places.json`/pool `note`s; y0/y1 left as the colophon states.
- The same painting on two books: Rembrandt's The Night Watch (both Dumas), Bazille's Self-Portrait (Maugham, Leblanc); two "Untitled" pairs (Bannister; unknown) are probably different works. Most-used painters: Sargent 16, J. Allen St. John 14, N. C. Wyeth 13, Monet 11.
- Oldest: Saint Anthony the Abbot in the Wilderness, unknown, 1435; van Eyck's Last Judgment, 1436. Newest genuine: Brillhart's The Survivors, 1958; Waring's Portrait of a Lady, 1947. By century: 1400s 3, 1500s 16, 1600s 47, 1700s 55, 1800s 620, 1900s 405 (plus 23 undated or century-only).
- Standard Ebooks' own artwork pages (`/artworks/<artist>/<work>`) carry the artist's death year and a US-PD rationale for every cover added since 2023; a future run could read them for the licence check instead of memory.
