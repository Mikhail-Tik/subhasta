## 20. v29: the audit acted on, the Eye, the School (November 2026)

**Why.** The owner was suspicious of how fast v28 arrived and asked for three things: a full audit (the minuses, what to tune, add, borrow and connect), a daily game in the manner of Anthropeum made better (a work a day on a map and a timeline, everyone competing on period and place), and an educational section that gives a newcomer ground under the feet — the archives and the data, the movement of money, the sanctions and the trade rules — with the tools to improve their own skills; all of it only after the previous message’s work had been checked.

**The audit (`scratchpad/v29/audit.json`, `audit.md`; the findings table in STRATEGY part seven).** An independent agent checked 42 citations (35 fully supported, 6 secondary entries that say more than their page, 0 contradicted, 1 unreachable), composed 25 briefs and scored them (mean 2.3 of 5), probed the engines, re-read the rules and the connections, and returned twenty findings. **The host’s limits, found by the audit and confirmed by the page contract.** The published artifact runs in a frame that loads no image, stylesheet, fetch or media from any other host, silently. Three things followed: the live feeds of v26 (Frankfurter rates, the Art Institute of Chicago and Cleveland holdings, Wikipedia) never worked in the artifact and now say nothing rather than pretend (their elements stay hidden); the National Gallery of Art’s pictures on the dossiers (IIIF) have never shown there; and the Eye’s pool had to be built from pictures the page carries itself. The print buttons were dead there too (`window.print` does nothing in the frame): `printable(title, node)` saves a self-contained HTML page through the downloads capability instead, and outside the artifact the dialogue opens as before (`inArtifact()`; `window.__hosted` lets a test play the frame).

**The Eye (`src/eye.js`, pasted at `/*__EYE__*/`; view `eye`).** Four games a day, five rounds each (the record praises five; Anthropeum’s ten drew the complaint), the same works for everyone: *Where & when* — a work, a pin on an engraved mini-globe (`eyeGlobe`: `glbPlate`/`glbPlateDraw`/`glbLight` on its own `G`, the inverse projection `toLatLon` for the click, drag to turn, wheel and buttons to zoom, arrows and Enter on the keyboard, the answer and the free radius drawn at the reveal) and a year on the dial (−3000 to 2026, BCE shown); 500 points for the place on `500·exp(−max(0, km − tol)/max(600, 4·tol))` where `tol` is the granularity of the record (a city 40–60 km free, a dynasty or a country hundreds), 500 for the date on `500·exp(−Δ/max(8, 0.09·(2026 − mid)))` where Δ is the distance to the museum’s own earliest–latest range (full marks inside it); a free *Look closer* zoom; *The hammer* — a sold lot’s catalogue line with the estimate where published, name the price with fees, `1000` within 10% then `exp(−(|ln r| − ln 1.1)/0.55)`; *The hand* — a detail (a seeded zoom origin) and four names of the same period, 300/200/100 on three tries; *The forger* — one of two objects of the same kind is catalogued by the Cleveland Museum of Art itself as a modern forgery (nine definite records), 200 a pair; it plays only where the museum’s pictures can show, so not in the artifact. The day: `eyeDay()` (UTC) → `EYE_DAYS[(day − EYE_EPOCH) mod n]` for Where & when (the generator’s fixed seeded shuffle of the {SE_N} Standard Ebooks paintings and the Library’s 221, five a day, {DAYS} days without a repeat); the other games walk a fixed permutation of their pool (`eyeShuffle` with mulberry32 seeded by the pool’s size, a window per day). After every round the card teaches: the museum’s own did-you-know or first sentence (`dyk`), the record and its page, the dossier, the tide, the lots in the Hammer. The result string (◆◈◇· by quarter), the streak and the best per game (`store eye.record`), the archive (any of the last sixty days replayed from the same seed, scored and kept, not posted), the week’s league among signed-in members (`db` collection `scores/{self}`, one document a member with the week’s totals by game; rules `scores` read `view`, `scores/{self}` write `interact`; `userRef.profiles` for the names). A picture that fails to load lets the round be passed without penalty. Exposed: `window.__EYE`.

**The pictures (`tools/eye_atlas.py`, `img/eye/aNNN.jpg`, `data/eye_atlas.json`).** The open collections’ pool (1,160 Cleveland and Chicago records with the museum’s place strings, placed by `tools/gen_research.py` through `EYE_GEO` — 1,823 keys of cities, countries, cultures, dynasties, kilns and peoples, the most specific key the string carries winning, `geo` naming it) stays in `EYE_POOL` for a page served from a file or a server, where the museum’s CDN answers; the artifact needs its own files, and the only image host this session could read was GitHub. So the harvest (`scratchpad/v29/se/se_harvest.py`): Standard Ebooks keeps every ebook in a public repository whose colophon names the cover painting (title, year, artist with a Wikipedia link) and whose `images/cover.source.jpg` is the painting as taken from Wikimedia Commons or a museum; the catalogue’s 129 pages were listed, {SE_HARVEST} paintings downloaded and resized, the painter’s country or city taken from the National Gallery of Art’s constituents file (V) or carried unconfirmed, the licence rule applied (the painter dead more than seventy years or the work made before 1930). `eye_atlas.py` packs them twenty to a sheet (two columns of 640 × 800 cells on the paper colour, JPEG q76), {SHEETS} sheets of about {SHEET_MB} MB, so the 511-file cap holds; a row’s `img` is `sheet:x,y,w,h` and `eyePicHtml` draws the cell on a `<canvas>` (`eyeDrawSheets`, one sheet image cached per page). The place of a Standard Ebooks painting is the painter’s city or country, said so on the card.
