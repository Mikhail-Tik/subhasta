## The five worst findings

1. **Critical — the Hammer’s 154 lots carry no source and no flag.** The lot page links Wikimedia and Google image searches, nothing else. The README’s ‘every figure … carries a verification flag and links its source’ is untrue of the terminal’s central table. Fix: add a source and a flag to every lot (71 already exist in the v25 research). A research run.
2. **Critical — the Brief silently substitutes the United States for any place the Navigator does not know.** An Almaty dealer’s Kandinsky is costed ‘from United States to United Kingdom’; a Tbilisi Pirosmani gets the US export regime (‘No export licence for works of art as such’); an Auckland buyer is charged ‘Use tax at home (Washington DC 6% to New York City 8.875%)’. `briefPlan`: `brJ(b.from)||brJ(b.where)||'us'`. Fix: refuse to compose money, walls and road for an unknown place and say so; then add the countries. Hours, then a research run.
3. **Critical — a closed road does not stop the memo.** For a designated buyer’s Kandinsky to Moscow the wall closes (correctly, with EO 14024, EAR Supp. 5, Schedule 3A), and the same memo then prices ‘Import VAT in Russia (20.0%) $4.74m’, tells the persona to ‘Register with the house before the sale’ and ‘Instruct a fine-art shipper early’, and drafts a letter asking the head of sale for ‘the shipping and insurance from New York to Moscow, with the export licence lead time (the terminal reads 4–14 weeks)’. README: ‘a closed road stops there’. Fix: when any gate is closed, compose the reading, the walls and the lawful course only. Hours.
4. **Critical — the fee engine contradicts the research.** `SCHED.christies` prices Christie’s at 28% to US$2m; the terminal’s own verified source (The Art Newspaper, 17 Feb 2026, read today) and its worked cases say 27% to US$1.5m/£1m, 22% to US$8m, 15%, since September 2025. US$780,000 against US$735,000 on a US$3m hammer, on 74 Christie’s lot pages, the Lexicon calculator, the Deal Room and the Undertow. Flagged ‘open’ by the 9 November engine audit and still open. Fix: one line, then generate the schedules from the bundle. Hours.
5. **Serious — the Navigator cites a page nobody opened, as ‘V’, on every ledger.** The premium line cites `sothebys.com/en/buy/buyers-premium`, a URL that occurs nowhere in the bundle (the v28 run records that the houses’ fee pages were not opened), and the premium is Sotheby’s whatever the house or room. Rule 1 of the common brief, broken inside the engine. Fix: cite the pages the research did open; let the Brief take the house from the input. Hours.

## The verdict

Where the terminal cites, it cites well: 35 of 42 sampled figures are on the cited page as written, the law and the cases rest on primary sources, and the Currents and the Walls are the real asset. But that strength sits in the research bundle, not in the engines that ‘solve’. The Brief composes a confident memo from wrong defaults — the United States for any city it does not know, Sotheby’s for every house, a 0% seller’s commission, UK and US playbooks for Berlin, Dubai, Mumbai and Mexico City — continues past its own closed wall into a shipping quote and a letter for a prohibited sale, cannot see ivory, screens no name, and prints ‘the median’ from one lot; the central table of 154 lots carries no source; the fee engine disagrees with the research it is built on; and the instrument that tells the reader how much is verified does not count the half of the page where most of the unverified lines are. The 25 briefs scored 2.28 of 5: right where the research is deep (New York, London, Paris, Milan, the sanctions files), silently wrong everywhere else. This is a well-sourced library with a memo generator in front of it, not yet a foundation for ‘deep, precise analysis of any request in the field’. What would make it one, in order: a guard that refuses to compose money, walls or road for any place, house, playbook or material the data does not hold, and says so; one generated table for fees and rules so the engine cannot contradict the bundle; the stop at a closed road; a source and a flag on every lot, and a few hundred lots in the band where the Circle’s clients actually trade; the UK and US sanctions lists and the company registers built in at a dated version so the ‘exposed’ and ‘agent’ buckets check something; outcomes for every charge the page names; and a verification instrument over every collection. Each is hours or a research run; until they are done the memo should say ‘the terminal does not hold this’ far more often than it does.
